# Why Is the Sky Blue, but Sunsets Are Red? - YouTube Short, ~45 sec

**[HOOK - 0:00]**
Sunlight is white. So why is the sky blue, and why does it turn red at sunset? It's the same reason.

**[0:06]**
White light is every color mixed together. When it hits the air, tiny gas molecules scatter short wavelengths, blue and violet, much more than long ones like red. *(Visual: light beam hitting molecules, blue bouncing off)*

**[0:16]**
That scattered blue comes at you from every direction, so the whole sky looks blue.

**[0:22 - Open loop]**
But wait: violet scatters even more than blue. So why isn't the sky purple?

**[0:27]**
Two reasons: sunlight contains less violet to begin with, and your eyes are much more sensitive to blue.

**[0:34 - PAYOFF]**
At sunset, light travels through far more air to reach you. Most of the blue gets scattered away along the path, and what's left is orange and red.

**[0:41 - CTA]**
Same physics, different distance. Follow for one-minute science every day.

---
**Fact check**
- Rayleigh scattering: scattering intensity is inversely proportional to the fourth power of wavelength.
- Sky isn't violet: lower violet intensity in sunlight plus human eye sensitivity peaking toward blue/green.
- Sunset: longer atmospheric path length removes blue light.
